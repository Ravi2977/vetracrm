Billing counter hardware: printers, scanners and more

The best billing software is slow on the wrong hardware. Here is what a reliable counter setup looks like.

By Vetra CRM22 November 20262 min read

A billing counter is only as fast as its slowest part. Here is the hardware most shops, pharmacies and distributors need, and what to look for.

Computer

A reliable desktop or all-in-one with an SSD makes a bigger difference than a fast processor. SSDs start up and search much faster than old hard disks.

Printers

  • Thermal receipt printer (2" or 3"): fast, quiet, no ink. Ideal for retail.
  • A4/A5 printer: for detailed GST invoices, B2B bills and reports.
  • Label printer: for printing your own barcode labels and price tags.

Barcode scanner

A USB scanner works for most counters. Choose a 2D scanner if you need to read QR codes or barcodes from phone screens. Wireless scanners help with bulky items and stock counts.

Cash drawer

A drawer connected to the receipt printer opens automatically when a bill is printed, which improves cash control.

Customer display (optional)

Shows items and totals to customers, improving transparency.

Power and backups

  • A UPS for the computer and printer
  • Automatic daily backups to cloud or an external drive
Check compatibility first

Before buying, confirm with your software vendor which printer models and paper sizes are supported. It saves a lot of frustration on day one.

Software that talks to hardware

Desktop software integrates most smoothly with printers, scanners, scales and drawers. Read desktop vs web applications. Vetra Billing works with standard barcode scanners and thermal printers, and our desktop app team builds custom hardware integrations.

Frequently asked questions

Thermal printer or A4 printer for billing?

Thermal receipt printers are fast and cheap to run for retail counters. A4 or A5 printers suit detailed B2B invoices. Many businesses use both.

Do I need a UPS at the billing counter?

Yes. A UPS prevents data loss and keeps billing running during short power cuts.
