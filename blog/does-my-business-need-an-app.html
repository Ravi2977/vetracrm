Does my business need a mobile app? 7 questions to ask

An app can be a great investment, or an expensive icon nobody opens. These questions help you decide.

By Vetra CRM22 November 20262 min read

Many business owners feel they should have an app. Sometimes that is right; sometimes a good website and WhatsApp do the job better and cheaper. Ask these seven questions first.

1. How often will people use it?

Apps shine when used frequently: weekly orders, regular bookings, daily field work. If customers interact with you once a year, a website is usually better.

2. Who is the user: customers or your team?

Internal apps for sales reps, delivery staff or technicians often have the clearest return, because usage is guaranteed and they replace paperwork and phone calls.

3. Do you need phone features?

Push notifications, camera, GPS, offline mode and barcode scanning work best in an app.

4. Will it save time or make money?

Write down the expected benefit: fewer phone orders, faster field reporting, more repeat purchases. If you cannot describe it, wait.

5. Can it connect to your existing systems?

An app that does not talk to your billing, CRM or inventory creates double work. Plan integrations from the start.

6. How will people find and install it?

For customer apps, plan how you will get installs: in-store QR codes, WhatsApp messages, offers for app orders.

7. Can you maintain it?

Apps need updates for new phone OS versions and store policies. Budget for ongoing maintenance.

Examples that work well

A distributor's retailer ordering app, a field sales order app, a clinic booking app, and a loyalty app for a retail brand.

Still unsure? Talk to our mobile app team. We will tell you honestly if a website would serve you better. See also app development cost.

Frequently asked questions

Is a website enough instead of an app?

For many businesses, a fast mobile-friendly website plus WhatsApp is enough. Apps make sense when people use your service frequently or need features like notifications, offline use or device access.

What are good app ideas for small businesses?

Repeat ordering for distributors' retailers, booking for clinics and salons, loyalty for retail brands, and field apps for sales and service teams are common examples.
