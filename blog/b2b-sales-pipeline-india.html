How to build a B2B sales pipeline for Indian businesses

A useful pipeline helps manufacturers, suppliers, wholesalers and service teams see what is happening with each buyer enquiry—and what should happen next.

A B2B sale may include several conversations before a buyer is ready to place an order. A pipeline makes those conversations easier to review by showing each opportunity’s current stage, owner and next action.

The exact stages should match how your business sells. A manufacturer may need time to confirm specifications and stock; a distributor may focus on product availability, quotation and payment. Start with a small number of clear stages and adjust them as your process becomes clearer.

A practical B2B pipeline structure

1. New enquiry

Record every new buyer enquiry, including the business name, contact details, source and the product or service requested. If leads arrive through connected channels such as IndiaMART, Google Ads or Meta Ads, use the lead source to understand where the opportunity began.

2. Contacted

Move the opportunity once someone from your team has contacted the buyer. Add a note about the conversation and set the next task—for example, a call back or a request for missing specifications.

3. Requirements confirmed

Use this stage when you understand what the buyer is looking for. Record relevant details such as product, quantity, delivery location or service scope in the customer notes so the team can prepare the next response.

4. Quotation sent

Prepare and send the quotation with the relevant products and terms. Keep the sent quotation connected to the opportunity and follow up if the buyer has questions or has not responded. If your CRM provides a quotation-view notification, use it as context for the next conversation.

5. Negotiation or decision pending

Record open questions, changes requested and the date you plan to check in. Do not leave a deal in this stage without a clear next step.

6. Won or closed

When the order is confirmed, mark it won and continue with stock, invoice and payment tracking. If it does not proceed, close the opportunity and record the reason when known. This keeps the active pipeline useful.

Track more than the stage

A stage alone does not tell the team what to do. For every active opportunity, keep these details easy to find:

  • The lead source and buyer’s contact information
  • The product, quantity or service requirements discussed
  • The last conversation or quotation sent
  • The next action, its owner and due date
  • The current stock, invoice or payment update when relevant

Use tasks and reminders to bring upcoming follow-ups back to the team’s attention. Keep notes short, factual and useful to the next person who may speak with the buyer.

Review your pipeline every week

Set aside time to review new leads, follow-ups due, quotations waiting for a response and opportunities that have not moved. Ask: does each active lead have a next action? Are any stages unclear? Are any enquiries waiting too long for a reply?

Keep the pipeline honest

A pipeline is useful when its stages reflect real progress. Move an opportunity forward only when the buyer interaction supports it, and close stale opportunities rather than letting them inflate the active list.
