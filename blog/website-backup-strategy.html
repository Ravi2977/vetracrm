Website and software backup strategy: the 3-2-1 rule

Backups are boring until the day you need one. Then they are the only thing that matters.

By Vetra CRM22 November 20262 min read

Servers fail, updates go wrong, people delete things by mistake, and ransomware happens. A good backup strategy turns a disaster into a short inconvenience.

The 3-2-1 rule

  • 3 copies of your data (the live one plus two backups)
  • 2 different storage types or providers
  • 1 copy off-site, away from your main server or office

What to back up

  • Database: orders, customers, invoices, content
  • Files: uploads, images, documents
  • Code and configuration: ideally in version control
  • Desktop software data: local databases on office PCs (see offline-first software)

How often

Match frequency to how much data you can afford to lose. If losing a day of orders is unacceptable, back up the database more often than daily.

Retention

Keep several restore points, for example daily for two weeks, weekly for two months and monthly for a year, so you can go back before a problem started.

Security

Encrypt backups, restrict who can access them, and keep at least one copy that cannot be modified or deleted from the live server.

Test restores

A backup you have never restored is a hope, not a backup. Restore to a test environment every quarter and confirm the site or software works.

Office PCs count too

Billing and accounting data often lives on one office computer. Schedule automatic backups to the cloud, not just a pen drive in the same drawer.

Our support plans include scheduled, tested backups. Related: maintenance checklist.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I back up my website?

It depends on how often data changes. A brochure site may need weekly backups; an e-commerce site or business software with daily transactions needs at least daily database backups.

Are my hosting provider's backups enough?

They help, but keep at least one independent copy elsewhere, because a hosting account problem can affect both the site and its backups.
