Moving from Excel to a CRM: when to switch and how to do it

Spreadsheets are a fine start, but they break as leads and team members grow. Here is how to know it is time, and a simple 30-day switch plan.

7 signs you have outgrown Excel

  1. Different people keep different versions of the sheet.
  2. Leads are added hours or days after they arrive.
  3. Nobody is reminded when a follow-up is due.
  4. You cannot see who changed what.
  5. Quotations, invoices and payments are tracked in other files.
  6. WhatsApp chats are not linked to the lead.
  7. Reports take hours to prepare.

Excel vs CRM

  • Lead capture: manual typing vs automatic from IndiaMART, ads and website
  • Follow-ups: memory vs reminders and alerts
  • Team access: shared file vs roles and permissions
  • History: overwritten cells vs full timeline
  • Reports: manual formulas vs live dashboard

A 30-day plan to switch

  1. Week 1: clean your sheet, remove duplicates and agree on lead statuses.
  2. Week 2: import leads and customers, connect lead sources and set up users and roles.
  3. Week 3: run new leads only through the CRM; set reminders and send quotations from it.
  4. Week 4: stop updating the spreadsheet and review the pipeline weekly in the CRM.
Key rule

Pick a cut-off date after which the spreadsheet is read-only. Running both in parallel for long means neither is accurate.

How to clean your spreadsheet before import

  • Use one row per lead or customer
  • Split names, phone numbers and emails into separate columns
  • Format phone numbers with country code
  • Remove duplicates and test entries
  • Map your old statuses to the new CRM statuses
  • Add a source column if you know where leads came from

Getting your team on board

Explain why you are switching: fewer missed leads and less reporting work. Show each person how the CRM makes their day easier, such as reminders and one-click quotations. Make the first week light: just log new leads and follow-ups. Add quotations and campaigns once the basics are a habit.

Mistakes to avoid when switching

  • Importing messy data and fixing it later
  • Creating too many custom fields on day one
  • Letting people keep updating the old sheet
  • Not connecting lead sources, so manual entry continues
