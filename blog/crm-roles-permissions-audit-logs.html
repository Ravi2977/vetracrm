CRM security: roles, permissions and audit logs explained

Your CRM holds your most valuable data: customers, prices and orders. Here is how to control who can see and change it.

Why CRM access control matters

Without access control, every user can export your full customer list, change prices or delete records. Good permissions protect against mistakes, misuse and data leaving with departing employees.

Setting up roles and permissions

  • Create roles that match real jobs: sales, sales manager, support, dispatch, accounts, admin
  • Give each role only the modules and actions it needs (view, create, edit, delete, export)
  • Limit lead visibility so sales people see only their assigned leads
  • Keep admin rights with as few people as possible

Login security

Use password plus OTP for most users, or OTP-only login for teams that share devices. Remove access the same day someone leaves.

Audit logs and activity logs

  • Audit log: who changed what and when
  • Login and authorization logs: who logged in, from where, and what they were allowed to do
  • User activity: every action by every user
  • Error and general logs: system issues for admins to review
Checklist

Review roles every quarter, check login logs after staff changes and investigate unusual exports.

Example role setup

  • Sales executive: view and edit own leads, create quotations, no export
  • Sales manager: view all leads, reassign, view reports
  • Support agent: view customers and orders, manage tickets, returns and refunds
  • Dispatch: view orders and update delivery status
  • Accounts: view orders, invoices and payments
  • Admin: full settings, users, roles and logs

Offboarding checklist when someone leaves

  1. Disable their login the same day.
  2. Reassign their open leads and tasks.
  3. Review their recent activity and exports in the logs.
  4. Change shared passwords for connected services if any were known to them.

When data control needs to go further

Some businesses need customer data stored on their own infrastructure for internal or customer policies. In that case a self-hosted CRM gives you control of the server, the database and backups, in addition to roles and logs.

Frequently asked questions

What are roles and permissions in a CRM?

Roles group users by job, and permissions define what each role can see and do, such as viewing, editing or exporting leads.

What is an audit log in a CRM?

An audit log records important changes, showing who made each change and when.
