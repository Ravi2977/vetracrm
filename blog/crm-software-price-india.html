How CRM software is priced

  • Per user per month: common with global CRMs. Costs grow quickly as your team grows.
  • Flat monthly plans: one price for a set number of users and features. Easier to budget.
  • One-time license: typical for self-hosted CRM. Higher upfront cost, no monthly subscription.
  • Free plans: useful for trying a CRM, but usually limited in users, automation and integrations.

Hidden costs to watch for

  • WhatsApp integration sold as a paid add-on
  • Automation, workflows or AI charged separately
  • Extra fees for IndiaMART or ad integrations
  • Paid onboarding and data migration
  • GST added on top of the listed price
  • Annual billing required to get the advertised price
Remember

WhatsApp Business API message charges are set by Meta and paid to the provider, whichever CRM you use. Budget for them separately.

What a fair CRM budget looks like

For a small team of 2 to 5 people, expect to spend a few thousand rupees per month on a CRM that covers leads, follow-ups and quotations. Growing teams that want WhatsApp chat, campaigns and automatic lead assignment usually spend more. Businesses that want AI agents, e-commerce integration and full logs sit at the top end.

If you plan to use a CRM for many years, or need data on your own servers, a one-time self-hosted license can cost less over time than a monthly subscription.

Example: comparing costs for a 6-person team

Say you have 6 users. A per-user CRM at ₹1,000 per user per month costs ₹6,000 a month before add-ons. If WhatsApp and automation cost extra, the real bill can be much higher. A flat plan that includes up to 10 users, WhatsApp chat and automation, such as Vetra Growth at ₹4,999, can cost less even though its headline price looks higher than a single user licence.

Always compare the total monthly cost for your actual team and the features you will really use.

One-time setup and onboarding costs

Many vendors charge for setup: configuring lead sources, importing data, creating templates and training users. This is often worth paying for because a well-configured CRM gets used. Ask for setup scope in writing: which integrations, how many records will be imported and how many training sessions are included.

Monthly vs annual billing

Monthly billing gives flexibility while you test the CRM with your team. Annual billing sometimes comes with a discount but locks you in. If you are new to CRM, starting monthly and moving to annual once the team is using it daily is a sensible approach.

Is a CRM worth the price?

Work it out with your own numbers. If your average order is ₹20,000 and a CRM helps you close just one extra deal a month by following up on time, it pays for itself many times over. Most businesses lose far more to forgotten follow-ups than they spend on software.
