For most patients, the lab report is the only part of your lab they see. A well-designed report makes results easy to read, highlights what needs attention, and reflects the quality of your work.

1. Header: identity and trust

  • Lab name, logo, address and contact details
  • Registration or accreditation details, if applicable

2. Patient and sample block

  • Patient name, age, gender and a unique ID
  • Referring doctor
  • Sample collection date and time, received time and report date
  • Sample type

Put this block in the same place on every report so doctors find it instantly.

3. Results table

TestResultUnitReference range
Haemoglobin10.8 Lg/dL12.0 – 15.5
Total leucocyte count7,400/µL4,000 – 11,000

Show results with units and the reference range for the patient's age and gender. Mark values outside the range clearly (bold, an H/L flag, or colour that also prints well in black and white).

4. Group related tests

Group tests under section headings (Complete Blood Count, Lipid Profile, Liver Function) in a consistent order.

5. Notes and methods

Add method notes or interpretive comments where they help, but keep them short.

6. Signatures

Include the name, qualification and signature of the authorised pathologist, and page numbers on multi-page reports.

Readability checklist

Clear fonts, enough spacing, results aligned in one column, flags easy to spot, and a layout that works on a phone screen as a PDF.
