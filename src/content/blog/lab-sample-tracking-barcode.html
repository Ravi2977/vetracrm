Sample mix-ups are rare but serious. Most start with something small: a handwritten label, a smudged name, or two patients with similar names on a busy morning. Barcode sample tracking removes most of these risks.

How barcode tracking works

  1. Registration: the patient and tests are entered in the lab software.
  2. Label printing: the software prints a barcode label for each tube type required.
  3. Collection: labels go on the tubes at the collection point, in front of the patient.
  4. Receipt: the lab scans tubes on arrival, recording time and condition.
  5. Processing: departments see their samples in a worklist; analyzers that support it read the barcode directly.
  6. Results: results attach to the right patient and test automatically.

Benefits

  • Fewer identification errors
  • Faster sample receipt and sorting
  • Clear status for every sample: collected, received, in process, reported
  • Accurate turnaround time measurement

What you need

  • Lab software with barcode support
  • A thermal label printer and tube labels
  • Barcode scanners at receipt and in departments

Home collection

For home collections, labels can be printed in advance against scheduled visits, or printed at the lab on receipt with the visit details checked carefully.

Rule of thumb

Label in front of the patient, never in advance in a batch at the desk. It is the simplest way to prevent mix-ups.
