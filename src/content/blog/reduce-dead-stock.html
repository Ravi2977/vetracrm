Every business has some products that stopped selling. The problem is not having a few slow items; it is not knowing which ones they are, and continuing to buy more of them.

1. Find it with an ageing report

Run a report of items with no sales in the last 90 or 180 days, sorted by stock value. Start with the items holding the most money.

2. Understand why it stopped selling

  • A newer model launched
  • Price is higher than competitors
  • Item is hidden on the shelf
  • It was over-ordered because of a scheme

3. Clear it

  • Discount: a clearance price recovers cash quickly
  • Bundle: pair slow items with best sellers
  • Return or exchange: check supplier terms
  • Move: transfer to a branch where it sells
  • Promote: send an offer to customers on WhatsApp

4. Stop it from coming back

Buy based on sales data, not habit or schemes. Set reorder levels using the reorder level formula, and review slow movers monthly.

Example

An electricals shop found ₹2.4 lakh in items with no sales in 180 days. Bundling switches with fast-selling wires and a clearance offer on old models recovered most of it within two months, and the owner stopped reordering five product lines.
