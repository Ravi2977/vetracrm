A typical medical store carries thousands of items from dozens of companies. Managing that inventory well is the difference between a healthy margin and a shop full of cash tied up in boxes.

Know your A, B and C items

Sort items by sales value over the last few months:

  • A items: a small share of SKUs that brings most of your sales. Never run out of these.
  • B items: steady sellers. Review regularly.
  • C items: the long tail. Keep minimal stock and order on demand.

Set minimum and maximum levels

For each important item, set a minimum (reorder) level and a maximum level. When stock falls below the minimum, it appears on your short list. Base levels on recent sales and how long your distributor takes to deliver. Our reorder level formula guide explains the calculation.

Use a daily short list

Instead of walking the shelves, generate a short list from the software every morning and send it to distributors. Add items customers asked for that you did not have; these missed sales are valuable signals.

Plan purchases around cash and schemes

  • Compare schemes against how fast an item really sells
  • Spread orders across distributors where it improves rates and availability
  • Check supplier outstanding before placing large orders

Control dead and slow stock

Run a report of items with no sales in the last 90 days. Return what you can, stop reordering, and avoid buying similar products. Combined with expiry management, this frees up cash fast.

Audit regularly

Physical stock drifts from system stock because of billing errors, unrecorded returns and breakage. Count high-value items every month and the full store once or twice a year, then correct the system.

Key numbers to watch

Stock value, stock value by company, items below minimum, items with no sales in 90 days, and expiry write-offs. Review them monthly.
