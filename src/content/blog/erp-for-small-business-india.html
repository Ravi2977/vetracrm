An ERP connects the main parts of a business, such as sales, purchase, inventory, production and accounts, so everyone works from the same data. For a growing small or mid-sized business, that can mean the end of re-typing orders, chasing stock figures and month-end surprises.

Signs you need an ERP

  • Orders, production and dispatch are tracked in separate Excel sheets
  • Nobody knows real-time stock without checking physically
  • The owner asks five people to get one number
  • Data is typed into multiple systems
  • Growth is limited because processes depend on a few key people

Common ERP modules

  • Sales: enquiries, quotations, orders, invoices (often linked to a CRM)
  • Purchase: requisitions, purchase orders, goods receipt
  • Inventory: stock by location, batches, transfers
  • Production: bills of material, work orders, consumption
  • Dispatch: packing, delivery, transport
  • Accounts: receivables, payables, or integration with Tally
  • Reports and dashboards for owners

Ready-made or custom ERP?

Ready-made ERPs suit standard trading businesses. Manufacturers with unique processes often find custom or customised ERPs a better fit, because the software follows their workflow rather than forcing changes. See custom vs off-the-shelf software.

Implementing an ERP successfully

  1. Map your current process and pain points
  2. Start with the modules that solve the biggest problems
  3. Clean and import master data (items, customers, suppliers)
  4. Train each department and appoint an internal owner
  5. Go live in phases and review monthly
Start small

Many successful SME ERPs begin with sales orders, inventory and dispatch. Production planning and advanced reports come once the basics are stable.
