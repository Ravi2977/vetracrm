For distributors, wholesalers and many retailers, credit sales are part of doing business. The problem starts when outstanding is tracked in a notebook or in someone's memory. Payments slip, follow-ups are awkward, and cash gets stuck.

Here is a simple system you can run with billing software and a few minutes a day.

1. Record every invoice and payment in one place

Outstanding is simply invoices minus payments. If both live in the same software, the balance is always correct. Record part payments against specific invoices so you know which bills are still open.

2. Set credit terms for each customer

Decide how many days of credit each customer gets, and an optional credit limit. When you bill, your software can warn you if a customer is over their limit or has overdue bills.

3. Review an ageing report every week

An ageing report groups outstanding by how old it is: 0–30 days, 31–60, 61–90 and over 90. Focus on the older buckets first; the longer a bill stays unpaid, the harder it is to collect.

4. Send reminders on a schedule

  • A friendly note a few days before the due date
  • A reminder on the due date with the invoice attached
  • A firmer follow-up and a call after the due date

Sharing the statement or invoice PDF on WhatsApp works well, because customers see it quickly and can forward it to their accounts person.

5. Make paying easy

Add your UPI ID or a payment link to invoices and reminders. The fewer steps for the customer, the faster you get paid. If you send quotations, a quotation with a payment link can collect advance payments too.

A 10-minute daily habit

Every morning, open the overdue list, send reminders for anything due today, and call the top three oldest balances. Small, regular follow-ups recover more money than one big push at month end.

6. Reconcile weekly

Match payments received in your bank and UPI statements with entries in your software. Unmatched payments usually mean a customer paid but it was not recorded, which leads to embarrassing reminders.
