An electronics or mobile shop carries two very different kinds of stock: high-value units like phones, TVs and laptops, and a long tail of low-value accessories. Both need careful control, for different reasons.

High-value units: track every serial number

For phones, TVs and appliances, record the serial number or IMEI when you buy and when you sell. This lets you:

  • Prove which unit a customer bought for warranty claims
  • Handle returns to suppliers correctly
  • Spot missing units during audits

More detail in IMEI and serial number tracking.

Accessories: barcodes and reorder levels

Chargers, cables, covers and earphones sell fast and in many variants. Use barcodes for quick billing and set reorder levels for best sellers so they never run out.

Purchases and supplier schemes

Record purchases with serial numbers, rates and schemes. Track supplier outstanding and price-drop claims where your distributors offer them.

Warranty and service

Search by serial number to see the purchase date and warranty status, and log service or replacement requests.

Multiple shops or a godown

Track stock per location and transfer between them, so you know where a specific model is before telling a customer it's out of stock.

Reports that matter

  • Fast and slow-moving models
  • Item-wise profit
  • Stock ageing for high-value items
Watch stock ageing

Electronics lose value as new models launch. An ageing report shows which units have been on the shelf too long, so you can discount or bundle them early. See how to reduce dead stock.
