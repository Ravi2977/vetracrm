An inpatient stay can involve dozens of charges: room rent, doctor visits, nursing, procedures, medicines, consumables, lab tests and more. When these are written on paper and added up at discharge, some are inevitably missed.

1. Admission and deposit

Record the admission with the patient's details, admitting doctor, bed category and payer (self, corporate or insurance/TPA). Collect and record the advance deposit.

2. Automatic daily charges

Room rent, nursing and fixed daily charges should post automatically based on the bed category and the number of days, including transfers between wards.

3. Charges from every department

Medicines issued by the pharmacy, lab tests, radiology, procedures and consumables should post to the patient's IPD account from the department that delivers them, not be entered later from paper.

4. Packages

For package procedures, apply the package rate and track items outside the package separately.

5. Interim bills

Share interim bills regularly so families know the running total and top up deposits in time.

6. Insurance and TPA

Record policy details, pre-authorisation amounts and approvals. Separate payable-by-insurer and payable-by-patient amounts at discharge.

7. Final bill and discharge

Review the bill, apply approved discounts, adjust deposits, and generate the final bill with the discharge summary.

Leakage checklist

Unbilled pharmacy issues, lab tests ordered verbally, consumables used in procedures, room upgrades not reflected, and uncontrolled discounts. A connected HMS addresses all five.
