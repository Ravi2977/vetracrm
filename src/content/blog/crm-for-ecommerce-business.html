Why e-commerce businesses need a CRM

Marketplaces and store builders handle checkout, but customer relationships end up scattered: support on WhatsApp, refunds in the payment gateway, tracking on the courier site and marketing in another app. A CRM keeps every customer's orders, chats and tickets together.

Order and delivery tracking

Connect tracking providers such as 17TRACK and USPS so shipment status updates in the CRM. Your team, and your AI agent, can answer “where is my order?” instantly on WhatsApp.

Returns and refunds without the gateway portal

Handle return requests and refunds from the CRM, with a clear status for each. Your team does not need to log in to the payment gateway to process routine refunds.

Build your store with a storefront API

A headless storefront API lets developers build a custom e-commerce website in Next.js, React or any framework, while products, stock, coupons and orders live in the CRM. You get full design control and one back office.

  • Products and inventory managed once, shown on the website
  • Website orders appear in the CRM with payments and tracking
  • Coupons created in the CRM work on the store
  • Support tickets linked to the order
Who it suits

Brands that have outgrown template stores and want a fast custom website without running a separate e-commerce back end.

The e-commerce customer journey in a CRM

  1. A customer browses your store and asks a question on WhatsApp; the AI agent answers.
  2. They order on your website; the order appears in the CRM with payment status.
  3. The order ships; tracking updates flow in from the courier.
  4. The customer asks “where is my order?”; the status is shared instantly.
  5. They request a return; the return and refund are handled from the CRM.
  6. A month later they receive a coupon for their next order.

Marketplaces vs your own store

Marketplaces bring traffic but own the customer relationship. Your own store, connected to a CRM, lets you keep customer data, run your own campaigns and build repeat business. Many brands use both, with the CRM as the home for their direct customers.

E-commerce metrics to watch

  • Repeat purchase rate
  • Return and refund rate by product
  • Average delivery time
  • Support tickets per 100 orders
  • Revenue from WhatsApp and email campaigns
