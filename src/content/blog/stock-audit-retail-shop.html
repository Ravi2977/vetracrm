A stock audit (physical stock verification) compares what is actually on your shelves with what your software says. Differences point to billing errors, unrecorded returns, damage or theft. Regular audits keep your stock numbers trustworthy.

Step 1: Plan the audit

  • Choose a time with low sales, such as early morning or a holiday
  • Divide the shop into zones and assign a team to each
  • Finish pending purchase entries and bills before you start

Step 2: Freeze movement

Avoid sales and receipts during the count, or record them separately so they can be adjusted.

Step 3: Count accurately

Use barcode scanners or printed count sheets. For serialised items, scan each serial number. Count each zone twice with different people if possible.

Step 4: Compare with the system

Enter counts into your software and generate a variance report: system quantity, physical quantity and difference.

Step 5: Investigate differences

Before adjusting, check common causes: wrong item billed, return not entered, purchase entered twice, wrong unit of measure, or damaged items set aside.

Step 6: Adjust and record reasons

Post stock adjustments with a reason for each. Over time these reasons show where your process leaks.

Step 7: Prevent the next gap

  • Train staff on the causes you found
  • Restrict who can edit or delete bills
  • Start cycle counts for high-value items
Cycle counts beat annual panic

Counting 20 high-value items every week is easier than counting 5,000 items once a year, and catches problems while they are small.
