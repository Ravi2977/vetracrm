Sales challenges for manufacturers

  • Many enquiries from IndiaMART, trade portals and ads, with different requirements
  • Detailed quotations with multiple products and quantities
  • Long decision cycles with samples and negotiation
  • Repeat orders from the same buyers
  • Coordination between sales, production and dispatch

A manufacturing sales workflow in a CRM

  1. Enquiry arrives from IndiaMART or an ad and is assigned to the right sales person.
  2. Requirement, quantity and delivery location are recorded.
  3. Sample or catalogue is shared and a follow-up is scheduled.
  4. Multi-product quotation is sent with a payment link.
  5. Advance payment places the order automatically.
  6. Dispatch and delivery are tracked; GST invoice is generated.

Features manufacturers need most

  • Multi-product quotations
  • Product catalogue with specifications and images
  • Inventory and purchase management
  • Order and delivery tracking
  • Role-based access for sales, dispatch and accounts
  • Repeat-order campaigns on WhatsApp and email
Tip

Tag leads by product category to see which product lines bring the most profitable orders.

Example: a pump manufacturer

A pump manufacturer in Coimbatore gets enquiries from IndiaMART, its website and dealers. In the CRM, enquiries are tagged by pump type and routed to product specialists. Sales people send multi-product quotations with technical specifications. Advance payments create orders automatically, the dispatch team sees them immediately and tracking updates are shared with the buyer on WhatsApp.

Managing repeat buyers and dealers

  • Keep each buyer's order history, prices and preferred products
  • Remind sales people before regular buyers usually reorder
  • Run WhatsApp and email campaigns for new products
  • Track outstanding payments per buyer

Data control for manufacturers

Product pricing, customer lists and dealer terms are sensitive. Use roles and permissions so sales people see only their leads, keep export rights limited and review audit logs. Manufacturers with strict data policies often prefer a self-hosted CRM on their own servers.
