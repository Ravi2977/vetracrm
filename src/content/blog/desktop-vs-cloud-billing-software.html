When you choose billing software, one of the first questions is whether it runs on your computer (desktop) or on the internet (cloud). Both can create the same GST invoice. The difference shows up in day-to-day work: how fast the counter moves, what happens when the internet goes down, and whether you can see sales when you are away from the shop.

What is desktop billing software?

Desktop software is installed on a Windows or Mac computer. Your data is stored on that computer or on a local server shared by computers in the same shop.

  • Works offline: billing continues even when the internet is down.
  • Fast: no waiting for pages to load during a rush.
  • Hardware friendly: thermal printers, barcode scanners and cash drawers connect easily.
  • Your responsibility: you need backups, and access from outside the shop is limited.

What is cloud billing software?

Cloud software runs on servers on the internet. You open it in a browser or app and log in.

  • Access anywhere: check sales, stock and outstanding from home or on the move.
  • Multiple locations: every branch works on the same live data.
  • No backups to manage: the provider handles hosting, updates and backups.
  • Needs internet: a slow or broken connection slows billing.

Which should you choose?

Choose desktop if

  • You run a busy counter and internet is unreliable
  • You bill with barcode scanners and thermal printers all day
  • You have one shop and are on site most of the time

Choose cloud if

  • You want to see the business from anywhere
  • You have more than one shop, godown or sales person on the road
  • You prefer not to manage backups and updates
