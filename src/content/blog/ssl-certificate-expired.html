An SSL/TLS certificate lets browsers connect to your website securely over HTTPS. When it expires, visitors see a full-page security warning, and most of them leave.

What happens when it expires

  • Browsers show a "Your connection is not private" warning
  • Visitors, enquiries and sales drop immediately
  • Apps and integrations that call your site may fail
  • Customers lose trust

How to fix it quickly

  1. Log in to your hosting panel or server
  2. Renew or reissue the certificate (many hosts offer one-click free certificates)
  3. Install it and restart the web server if needed
  4. Check the site in a private browser window

Why this matters more now

Maximum certificate lifetimes are being reduced in stages under browser industry rules: down to about 200 days from March 2026, with further reductions planned over the next few years. Manual renewal once a year no longer works; automation is essential.

How to prevent it

  • Automate renewal using your host's built-in SSL or an ACME client
  • Monitor expiry with alerts several weeks in advance
  • Keep DNS and domain current, because renewal often depends on them
  • Document where certificates are used: website, API, mail server, subdomains
Don't forget subdomains

Certificates for subdomains such as crm.yourcompany.com or api.yourcompany.com can be separate from the main site, and are often forgotten.
